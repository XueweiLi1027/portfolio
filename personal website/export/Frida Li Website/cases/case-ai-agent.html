Does AI Interview Agent Work? An Agent Usability Study

After building a five-step Research Agent prototype in an internal AI hackathon, I designed and ran a 1:1 usability study with six senior PMs to find out whether the workflow held up before beta. The study surfaced the design and engineering bugs that turned an experimental prototype into a viable workflow tool, and seeded a confident beta launch with 32% PM adoption in 30 days.

01Background & Problem

In December 2025, I built a five-step Research Agent prototype during Anker's internal AI hackathon: a multi-agent system that takes a research goal and walks it end-to-end through plan → recruit → interview → insight, with synthetic users substituting for real participants in early-stage discovery.

The prototype was technically working but unproven. Senior PMs are the intended users, and I didn't yet know how they would actually use it: what mental model they'd bring, where they'd lose trust, where they'd want to step in. Shipping to beta without testing risked an experimental prototype landing on 160+ PMs' desks and dying quietly.

Primary research question
Where does the five-step Research Agent break down for senior PMs, and which bugs are blockers for beta versus things we can patch after?

02Approach

One week, sole researcher, sole engineer-partner. The fastest path to actionable signal was a small-N usability study with the right participants and the right tasks. The design choices that mattered:

Choice 01 · Participants
Senior PMs with real interview experience.
Six senior PMs, each of whom had personally run user interviews on real projects. They knew what a good interview guide and a good insight report looked like, which meant their reactions were diagnostic rather than exploratory. Recruited via internal announcements and group chats.
Choice 02 · Tasks
Replicate a study you've done.
Five tasks per participant, and each PM was asked to replicate a study they'd already run themselves. Real goals, real context, real ground truth. No hypothetical scenarios, no synthetic personas to imagine. The PMs could compare the Agent's output against what they'd actually delivered.
Choice 03 · Format
60-minute 1:1 sessions.
Long enough to run the full five-step pipeline, short enough to schedule six in one week. Think-aloud throughout, with each stage's output reviewed before moving to the next so we could catch where trust eroded in real time.
Choice 04 · Analysis & share-out
Problem statements, not vibes.
Problem-statement analysis: every issue was written as who · where · what · why it matters, then sorted by stage. A one-pager went to my partner engineer so design fixes and prompt-engineering fixes could ship in parallel.

03Findings · Design

Both blocker-level design issues sat in the back half of the pipeline (stages 4 and 5), and both came down to the same underlying gap: the Agent had been built as a black box, and senior PMs are not comfortable acting on a black box.

Finding 01 · Lack of control Stage 4 · Interview Agent
Users felt sidelined during the interview.
"It's running an interview I can't redirect, so it's not really my interview anymore."
Problem
The design was passive. Once the interview started, PMs could not interrupt, redirect the conversation, or ask a follow-up. Accountability evaporated: if the synthetic user said something off, no one had asked the next question.
Fix
Interactive Mode. PMs can pause the interview at any point and inject a follow-up or redirect. The Agent picks up the new thread and continues.
Viewer → Director
Why this matters: turns the Agent from something PMs watch into something they moderate. Same five-step pipeline, but the human is back in the loop where their expertise belongs.
Finding 02 · Lack of transparency Stage 5 · Insight Agent
Users wouldn't base decisions on unverifiable insights.
"I can't take this to my VP if I can't show where it came from."
Problem
The Insight Agent produced a clean summary report, but with no reference back to the raw interview data. Senior PMs refused to use any claim they couldn't trace. The report was a dead end.
Fix
Source-labeling. Footnotes on every claim in the report. Clicking a source reveals the verbatim quote in the transcript, with the synthetic user it came from.
Traceability → Trust
Why this matters: trust in a research output comes from being able to interrogate it. Source-labeling turned the report from "the Agent says so" into "here's the data, with the synthesis on top."

04Findings · Engineering

The other half of the report went to my partner engineer. Two categories of prompt-engineering issues, both invisible to whoever built the prototype and both immediately visible to users.

Bug class 01
Latency
  • Slow responses at multiple stages, so PMs lost focus while waiting
  • Too many rounds of conversation per stage: the Agent over-clarified instead of acting
Bug class 02
Instruction adherence
  • The Agent asked completely irrelevant questions inside the interview stage
  • Omitted explicit instructions in the system prompt
  • Asked the PM a question the PM had already answered, back at them

Both classes ended up in the engineer's next sprint. The point of running the usability study with senior PMs was that they could tell the difference between "the Agent is being unhelpful because the workflow is wrong" and "the Agent is being unhelpful because the prompt is wrong", and routing those to design vs. engineering separately made the fix tractable for a one-week turnaround.

06What I Did

I was the only researcher on this project, working alongside a single partner engineer. I both built the thing and evaluated it.

  • Built the prototype. Created the five-step Research Agent during Anker's internal AI hackathon, the multi-agent pipeline the study went on to evaluate.
  • Designed the study. Recruited six senior PMs with real interview experience and set the replicate-a-study-you-have-run task structure so their feedback was diagnostic rather than hypothetical.
  • Ran every session. Moderated all six 60-minute 1:1 think-aloud sessions in a single week, reviewing each pipeline stage before moving to the next.
  • Analyzed the issues. Wrote each problem as a who / where / what / why-it-matters statement and sorted them by pipeline stage to separate blockers from patches.
  • Drove the fixes. Packaged everything into a one-pager, routing design issues to my own next iteration and prompt-engineering issues to my partner engineer so both shipped in parallel.

07Lessons & Reflection

Two lessons I carry forward from this study: one methodological, one about the nature of agentic systems.

01 · Real-life scenarios beat hypothetical tasks for agentic evaluation

Hypothetical tasks ("imagine you're researching X") add a layer of uncertainty: was the Agent bad, or was my imagined scenario unfair to it? Asking PMs to replicate a study they had actually run removed that layer entirely. Expert users compared the Agent's output against ground truth they already knew, and gave objective feedback on agent performance instead of trying to imagine what feedback would be fair.

Method takeaway
For agentic UX work specifically: real-life scenarios free expert users from imagination work. Hypothetical tasks make sense for novice users in greenfield concept testing; expert users in evaluative studies should always replicate their own real work.

02 · Black-box pipelines lose senior users at the same two places

The two design bugs, no control during interview and no traceability in the report, are not specific to this Agent. They're structural failure modes of any multi-agent pipeline that hands an expert user a clean output without showing the work. Building Viewer-to-Director control hooks and source-labeling into the spec from day one would have saved us a full design iteration.

03 · Separating design bugs from engineering bugs is the trick

What looks like one bad agent is usually two different bugs in two different shops. Routing the design problems to my own next iteration and the prompt-engineering problems to my partner engineer's sprint, in the same one-pager, is what made the one-week turnaround actually deliverable.
