About Me

I'm Frida Li (she/her), a social scientist and user researcher with 2+ years of experience across health wearables, e-commerce, and research workflow scaleability. I'm now looking for my next role in UX research, consumer insights, or strategy and operations.

Most recently, I was the sole researcher at Anker Innovations, where I led research for their health wearable team that directly shaped the product roadmap. [see this case for example] Alongside that, I built the company's research infrastructure from scratch, including a 70,000-user panel with 100+ behavioral and attitudinal user tags, and a Claude Code-powered Research Agent that lets product teams run synthetic user interviews for early-stage concept testing and pain point discovery (32% adoption across 25 product teams in 30 days).

Before Anker, I spent time at TikTok Shop, leading research across Southeast Asia, where my projects touched on creator commerce, user shopping experience, and localized shopping behavior. My findings directly shaped creator strategy and product direction. [see this case for example]

On the side, I'm a licensed English-Mandarin interpreter and have spent 6+ years coaching Chinese students through the IELTS, the English proficiency exam required for university admission in most English-speaking countries. My students range from college seniors to seasoned professionals making a mid-career leap, and getting to learn from each of them has been one of the best things in my life.

I'm currently based in Seattle, after having lived in Chicago, Shanghai, Melbourne, Canberra, and Shenzhen. San Francisco and New York are next on the list, if life permits.

University of Chicago · 2024
How does Professional Background Shape Legislative Behavior in China's National People's Congress?

China's National People's Congress is often dismissed as a rubber stamp, yet its delegates freely choose which bills to sponsor. What does professional background predict about that choice? Across 622 delegates and 2,209 bills, functional delegates (doctors, lawyers, officials) increasingly sponsor bills in their own field as they gain seniority, while CEOs and party cadres drift beyond theirs. The finding shows two distinct strategies for winning influence without elections.

Content Analysis Logistic Regression Authoritarian Politics Legislative Behavior
University of Chicago · 2024
What Drives Housing Prices in U.S. High-Poverty Neighborhoods? A Computational Social Science Study

In high-poverty neighborhoods across NYC, LA, and Chicago, it was unclear whether a home's own attributes or its surrounding neighborhood resources better explained its price. Neighborhood attributes mattered most overall, with each city showing distinct preferences: Chicago favored single-family residential, LA weighted business atmosphere, and NYC discounted hospital proximity.
